The drive

Munich Airport to Bolzano, 300 km

A8, A93 and A12 to Innsbruck, then the A13 climbs to the Brenner Pass at 1,370 m and becomes the Italian A22 on the far side. From there it is a long descent down the Eisack valley through Brixen and Klausen to Bolzano at just 262 m. Around 300 km, about 3 h 15, almost entirely motorway.

The Brenner is the lowest of the main Alpine crossings and the busiest freight route in the Alps. It stays open in essentially all conditions, but heavy-goods traffic can slow the climb — which is why the time is 3 h 15 rather than the 2 h 45 the distance suggests.
South Tyrol, Italy

About Bolzano

South Tyrol is Italian by passport and Austrian by almost everything else — it was ceded in 1919 and still speaks German first. Bolzano sits in a bowl of vineyards where three valleys meet, with palm trees in the square and the Dolomites half an hour away. The city is the base for the whole region: Val Gardena, Alta Badia and Kronplatz are all within an hour and a half, and the Dolomiti Superski pass covers twelve areas on one ticket. Bolzano itself is worth a day for Waltherplatz, the arcaded Laubengasse, and Ötzi the iceman in the archaeology museum.
